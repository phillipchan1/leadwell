# StoryBrand (SB7) — LeadWell BrandScript

> **Status:** DRAFT — the internal-problem and success language especially needs
> validation against real leader interviews. See
> [`../research/README.md`](../research/README.md).

Donald Miller's frame, applied. The discipline it enforces: **the customer is
the hero, we are the guide.** Every piece of copy in the app, every landing
page, every AI system prompt should be checkable against this. If LeadWell is
the hero of a sentence, that sentence is wrong.

---

## 1. A Character (who the hero is and what they want)

**Who:** A leader who is responsible for people in more than one context — a
staff team, a volunteer team, a peer group, a family — and who reports up to
someone in most of those contexts.

**What they want:**

> *"I want the people I lead to be genuinely better off for having been led by
> me — and I want to stop feeling like I'm winging it."*

The surface want is *be more organized about people*. The real want is
**integrity between who they intend to be as a leader and how they actually
show up on a Tuesday.**

## 2. Has a Problem

This is the heart of the framework — three layers, and the internal one is what
actually sells.

**Villain** (personify the enemy — one thing, concrete):
> **Drift.** The slow, invisible erosion of attention. Nobody decides to
> neglect someone. Drift decides for you. It picks off the quiet and competent
> first, because they never generate an alert.

**External problem** (the tangible obstacle):
> I lead dozens of people across four unrelated contexts, and the knowledge
> about them lives in my head, my phone notes, three Google Docs, and a
> CliftonStrengths PDF I opened once.

**Internal problem** (how that makes them *feel* — the reason they'd pay):
> *"I'm afraid I'm a shallower leader than I believe myself to be. I can't
> remember what we talked about last time. Someone on my team is struggling
> right now and I don't know who."*

Secondary internal notes to test: guilt about the volunteer team getting the
leftovers of the day job; anxiety that leading up is a political game they're
losing without knowing the rules.

**Philosophical problem** (why this is *just wrong*):
> People should not have to be the loudest person in the room to get their
> leader's attention. If someone gives you their working life, they deserve to
> be led by someone who actually knows them.

## 3. And Meets a Guide

The guide needs two things: **empathy** and **authority**.

**Empathy** — say this, roughly:
> "You're not disorganized. You're carrying more relationships than any human
> memory was built for, in more directions than any single tool accounts for."

**Authority** — what earns the right to guide:
- Built on established frameworks the leader already trusts and may already
  have results from — CliftonStrengths (all 34 themes, mapped to the four Gallup
  domains), Enneagram, MBTI. We didn't invent a personality theory.
- Models the four real capacities of leadership — formal authority, leadership
  without authority, peer influence, and leading up — rather than pretending
  everything is a reporting line.
- Takes leading up seriously as a discipline with its own artifacts, which
  almost no tool does.

> ⚠️ Authority we do **not** have yet: testimonials, a named practitioner
> behind it, usage numbers. This is the weakest slot in the script today. Fill
> it before any public launch.

## 4. Who Gives Them a Plan

**The Process Plan** (what they do — keep it to three steps, always three):

1. **Map your world.** Put every team and person on one canvas — work, church,
   family — and mark what capacity you hold with each.
2. **Load what you know.** Dump assessments, history, and gut-feel into each
   profile. Brain-dump in plain language; it gets structured for you.
3. **Lead from it.** Before every 1:1, meeting, or hard conversation, get a
   specific read on that specific person.

**The Agreement Plan** (what we promise, to defuse fear):

- Your workspace is private. The people in it never see it and never will
  without your explicit act.
- The AI reads only your data, and it works for you, not for your company.
- Nothing here is a performance record. It is a leadership aid.
- Your data is yours; you can take it out.

## 5. And Calls Them to Action

**Direct CTA:** *Map the people you lead* — (sign in with Google, start with
your first team)

**Transitional CTA** (for those not ready — currently missing, build one):
- *"The Leading-Up Manual"* — a one-page template for figuring out what your
  boss actually rewards. Free, useful standalone, and it teaches our worldview.
- *"The Drift Audit"* — five questions that reveal who on your team you've gone
  quiet on.

## 6. That Helps Them Avoid Failure

What's at stake if nothing changes:

- The quiet high performer resigns and you are genuinely surprised. That
  surprise is the whole indictment.
- You give the same generic feedback to a Deliberative and an Activator and
  wonder why only one of them moved.
- Your volunteer team runs on your guilt instead of your attention, and burns
  out the two people carrying it.
- You get passed over and can't articulate a single delivered win in your
  manager's language, because you never wrote them down.
- Five years of leadership experience turns out to be one year, repeated five
  times, because nothing accumulated.

## 7. And Ends in Success

- You walk into every conversation already knowing what matters.
- The quiet ones get seen on purpose, not by luck.
- You lead the same person differently at church than at work — deliberately,
  because the capacity is different, not because you forgot.
- Your relationship with your own boss stops being weather and starts being
  something you manage.
- Your leadership compounds. Year five is genuinely better than year one,
  because it's built on a record instead of a memory.

## Character transformation

The single most important line in the script. Who does the hero *become*?

| From | To |
|---|---|
| A well-meaning leader running on memory and guilt | A leader with a system, who knows their people on purpose |
| Reactive — attention goes to whoever escalates loudest | Intentional — attention is allocated, and the quiet are seen |
| Leading everyone the same way | Leading each person the way *they* are built to be led |
| Managing up by instinct and hope | Managing up with a model and a record |

## Derived copy

**One-liner** (the thing you say at a dinner party):
> *Most leaders carry more people than memory can hold, so the quiet ones drift.
> LeadWell keeps everyone you lead — at work, at church, anywhere — in one map
> with a coach that actually knows them, so nobody falls through.*

**Website/app header hierarchy:**
- H1: **Lead every person like you actually know them.**
- Sub: Work, church, volunteers, and the people you report to — one map, one
  memory, one coach.
- CTA: Map the people you lead

**Copy rules that fall out of this script:**
- Never say "manage your people." They are not inventory. Say *lead*.
- Never position the AI as the hero ("our AI analyzes…"). Say what the leader
  gets: *"know what to say before you walk in."*
- Failure language stays in the second person and stays concrete — *"the quiet
  ones drift"*, not *"suboptimal engagement outcomes."*
- Never imply we evaluate the leader. We are the guide; they are never judged
  by us.
