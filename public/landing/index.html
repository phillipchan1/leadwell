09:00People · who you lead

Nobody opens the chart wanting everything. You open it in a mode.

  1. View · Circles

    Rings around you.

    Everyone you lead, drawn as rings around you: the teams, and the people on them.

  2. View · Tree

    Or as the org tree.

    An infinite canvas you can pan, zoom and rearrange. Whoever you report to sits above you, and a table view sits beside it for comparing.

  3. 1 Plan

    What is each team for, and what’s next?

    Mandate and next step are team-level answers, so Plan leaves the roster off.

  4. 2 Prep

    Am I ready for the next 1:1?

    A rhythm ring on every person and a countdown on every meeting, worst first.

  5. 3 Assess

    Is this going well? Only you can say.

    Health is one call you make yourself, with a line of evidence behind it. It’s never derived, and after ninety days it says stale. Scan for one level and everything else dims, so the strain shows up inside the shape of the org.

  6. 4 Pray

    Who am I carrying?

    A flame on everyone you’re praying for, dashed on the ones who’ve gone quiet.

10:45Profiles

Read each person the way the assessments already do.

Record someone’s CliftonStrengths top five, Enneagram and MBTI. Strengths show in Gallup’s own domain colors, so a chip here matches every report your people already have. Teams show their domain balance and their blind spots.

Alongside the profile: strengths, watch-outs, how to lead them, goals, dated notes and a topic board. There’s also an AI coach for each person.

11:56Readiness · meetings

You’re sitting down in four minutes. Do you know what this meeting is for?

  • On the booksa next one exists, booked or projected
  • Last one written up
  • Agenda has something queued
  • No overdue commitments

Give a meeting a rhythm and Polarch projects the next one from the last, so nothing has to be on a calendar for the signal to work.

  1. DriftingPast the rhythm, nothing next.
  2. Loose endMet, never written up.
  3. Prep dueWindow open, checklist not done.
  4. ReadyNothing to do. Show up.
  5. RestingMet recently. Nothing owed.
  6. DormantAs-needed, floor not crossed.

Worst first, on purpose: not having seen someone outranks not having filed the notes. Cards roll up worst-of, never an average.

17:00Leading up

Leading down, you track their growth. Leading up, you track your value.

A board, a leadership team, the one leader you answer to: they sit above you in the tree, with the same check-ins, notes and topic board as everyone else. A topic for your boss is an ask, an escalation or a decision you need.

18:20A basement, a plane, a hallway with two bars

Walking in with ninety seconds to spare? Press ⌘K.

One field over the whole workspace. Search lands you in the write-up, note or prayer entry where something was said, not on a profile you then have to dig through. It never touches the network.

  • Works offline

    Fonts, shell and your data are already on the device.

  • Synced everywhere

    Sign in with a link to your email and your workspace follows you.

  • Private by design

    Every account is its own workspace, isolated by row-level security.

  • Keyboard first

    C to capture, G then a letter to jump, ? for the rest.

    21:30Prayer · who you’re carrying

    There is no scale, and there is no score.

    Taking someone up in prayer is a deliberate act, and so is laying them down. Polarch only measures the one honest thing it can: how long it’s been.

    • Prayed this weekFilled.
    • CarryingOutlined.
    • Gone quietDashed, after 21 days. The only one that asks anything of you.
    • AnsweredGilt. Never “done”.

    Praying for four people out of forty is a true answer. Not carrying is never counted as a gap.
